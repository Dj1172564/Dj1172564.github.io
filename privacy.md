# Recimama Privacy Policy

*Last updated: 28 September 2026*

Recimama is a cooking app made by David Rodriguez ("we", "us"). This policy describes what the app does with your information: what stays on your device, what goes to your own iCloud, what becomes public if you publish a recipe, and what other websites receive when you ask the app to fetch something.

**In short:** Recimama has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and it contains no third-party code. Your recipes stay on your device and in your own private iCloud, which we cannot see. We can see something about you in only two situations: when you **publish a recipe to Explore**, which makes it public, and when you **report something or email us**.

---

## 1. What is kept on your device

Recimama keeps these on your iPhone or iPad. If you're signed in to iCloud, they are also copied to your own private iCloud, unless you turn sync off (section 2):

- your recipes, including their photos, notes, ratings and tags, and your recipe collections
- your meal plans, pantry and saved menus (shopping lists are worked out from your plans)
- your cooking history (for example, when you cooked a recipe and how long it actually took)
- your meal rotas and dinner parties, including the sign-ups on them (section 5)

These stay on this device only:

- which shopping-list items you've ticked off
- the list of Explore recipes and cooks you have hidden or blocked
- your agreement to the community guidelines
- the name you last typed when publishing, so you don't have to type it again

The app's widgets, its Siri shortcuts and its Share and Safari extensions read a small summary of this (what's cooking, what's planned for today, recipes you've clipped). That summary lives in a storage area shared only by Recimama's own parts on your device. If you have a paired Apple Watch, the step you're on and the steps coming next are sent straight to the watch while you cook, through Apple's WatchConnectivity.

## 2. Your private iCloud

**Settings > Storage > Sync with iCloud** is on unless you turn it off. While it's on and you're signed in to iCloud, everything in the first list in section 1 (your recipes and collections, plans, pantry, saved menus, cooking history, and your rotas and their sign-ups) is copied to your **private iCloud database** so it appears on your other devices. That database belongs to your Apple Account. We can't read it, and nobody else can unless you share a rota from it (section 5). Apple stores it under Apple's own terms and privacy policy.

Turning the switch off stops this copying from the next time you open the app, and straight away stops new rotas being put in iCloud automatically (section 5). What is already in iCloud stays there until you delete it (section 12). A rota you have already shared stays shared, so the people on it aren't cut off, until you stop sharing it. If you tap **Share > Invite people > Invite with a link** while the switch is off, that one rota is put in iCloud, because that is what the button is for. The switch doesn't affect publishing to Explore (section 3).

## 3. Explore: what becomes public when you publish

Explore is where people share recipes with everyone who uses Recimama. **Nothing is published unless you choose to.** For each recipe you publish, you open its menu, choose **Share with everyone**, agree to the community guidelines (asked once), and tap **Share this recipe**. Publishing needs you to be signed in to iCloud; browsing doesn't.

**What is published.** The recipe is saved in Recimama's **public iCloud database**, which Apple hosts for us. The record contains:

- the recipe's title, description, servings, ingredients (as written, with any notes), method steps and each step's timing and oven temperature, tags, emoji and card colour
- its total and hands-on time and how many steps it has
- **the card's photo**, if the recipe has one and it isn't a picture downloaded from a website. The app redraws every photo you add, which removes the location and camera details that phones store inside photos.
- **your name**, if "Your name (optional)" has one in it when you tap Share this recipe (up to 40 characters). After your first publish, that box starts with the name you used last time, so clear it if you don't want your name on this recipe. If it's empty, the recipe shows as "Someone".
- **your note**, only if you type one in "One line about it (optional)" (up to 200 characters)
- the date you published it, and small technical details the app uses to read the recipe back (for example, how the recipe was made, such as typed or dictated, and a format version)
- **an identifier for your iCloud account.** iCloud adds this to every public record automatically. It is a random code that iCloud creates for your Apple Account within Recimama. It doesn't contain your name, email address or phone number. It is the same on every recipe you publish, though, so it links them together. Recimama uses it so people can block a cook and so we can act on reports.

**Who can see it.** Anyone using Recimama can see your published recipe, including people who aren't signed in to iCloud, and anyone can add a copy to their own library. We can also see everything in the public database, including the iCloud identifier.

**What you can't publish.** Only recipes you wrote yourself can be published. The app refuses recipes imported from websites, recipes read from a photographed page, recipes that came from Explore or from another person, and recipes written by following a recipe published on a website. Before anything is published, the app checks its text for words Explore doesn't allow, on your device, and refuses if it finds one. Nothing is sent when a recipe is refused.

**Taking it down.** In the recipe's **More** menu choose **Shared — manage it**, or go to **Explore > More > Recipes you shared**. Swipe left on the recipe, tap **Take down**, then confirm with **Take it down**. The recipe stops appearing in Explore for everyone. Copies that people have already added to their own libraries stay with them. We can't reach those, and neither can you.

**If you can't take it down yourself** (for example, you have a new phone), email **[rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com)** with the recipe's title and roughly when you published it. We'll delete it, usually within 24 hours. If you'd like us to remove *everything* you've published, tell us, and we'll delete all the public records carrying your iCloud identifier.

## 4. Reports and blocks

**Reporting.** Every Explore recipe has a **Report** button. A report records which recipe you reported, the reason you chose, any comment you add (up to 200 characters), the time, and the identifier of the cook who published it. It is saved to the public iCloud database, where only we can read it; other users can't. iCloud also attaches **your** iCloud identifier to it, as it does to anything saved there.

If the report can't be sent (for example, you're offline), the app offers to **email** it to [rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com) instead. That email comes from your own mail account, so we'll see your email address and anything you add. We use these details only to act on the report.

**What we do with reports.** We review every report within 24 hours. If a recipe breaks the [community guidelines](guidelines.html), we delete it. If a cook keeps breaking them, we add their iCloud identifier to a **blocked list**, which Recimama checks so their recipes are hidden for everyone. The blocked list contains identifiers only, no names.

**Blocking a cook.** **Block** hides everything that cook publishes on your device, and also sends us a report saying you blocked them, so we can spot someone many people are blocking. **Hide this** hides one recipe on your device and tells nobody. Your blocked and hidden lists stay on your device. You can undo them from Explore's **More** menu.

## 5. Meal rotas and dinner parties

A meal rota (people taking turns to bring meals) or a dinner party ("A meal with friends") reaches the people you send it to, and anyone they pass it on to. We never see any of it.

- **Live sharing (needs iCloud).** If you're signed in to iCloud and **Sync with iCloud** is on, the app puts every rota you organise into your private iCloud database and creates a sharing link for it: when you first save the rota, and when you open a rota you made earlier. With the switch off it does this only for a rota you ask to share with **Invite with a link** (section 2). Nobody can open the rota until the link leaves your device, and it leaves in three ways: **Share > Send the link**, **Share > Invite people > Invite with a link** (called **Add someone to it** once the rota is live), and any invitation message you send or copy while the rota is live (next point). **Anyone who has the link can open the rota and sign up**, so send it only to people you mean to invite, and remember it can be forwarded. Everyone on the rota can see what you entered: who it's for, the dates, how many each meal feeds, what the household can't eat, your notes and requests, and every sign-up (each person's name, dish, note and the dish's ingredient names, which are used to check it against what the household can't eat). **Share > Manage who's on it** opens Apple's sharing screen, where the organiser can see and remove the people who joined, and stop sharing. It may show participants' Apple Account names. Once you stop sharing, the app doesn't share that rota again unless you invite people to it again.
- **An invitation message** (Share > Invite people, then **Send the invitation** or **Copy the message**) says who the meals are for, the open days or courses, how many each meal feeds, what the household can't eat, anything else you've asked people to bring, and your note. Unless the rota has finished, it ends with a link:
  - **If the rota is live, the link is its sharing link.** Anyone who gets the message, or has it forwarded to them, can open the whole rota, including everyone's names and sign-ups, and can sign up themselves.
  - Otherwise, it's an Recimama link that carries the rota's title, note, dates, servings, dietary needs, courses and requests, but no sign-ups.
- **A rota file** (Share > Send the rota) contains the whole rota, including everyone's sign-ups.
- **Copy as a message** is plain text you paste wherever you like. It gives the rota's name, what the household can eat, how many it feeds and your note, then each day with the name and dish of whoever signed up for it.

These go wherever you send them (Messages, Mail, AirDrop and so on), and the recipients' apps handle them under their own terms.

## 6. Recipe cards and backups you send

**Share recipe**, **Make a card** and **Settings > Backup > Export a backup** create files (a recipe file, an image or PDF, or a backup of your whole library). They go only where you send or save them, and we never receive them.

## 7. When the app goes online for you

Apart from iCloud, the app connects to other websites only when you ask it to, and only to do what you asked:

| When you… | The app contacts… | What it sends |
|---|---|---|
| use **Find one on the web** (the in-app browser) | DuckDuckGo if you type words (with "recipe" added unless you typed it), or the site you type or tap | your search or the address, as any browser would |
| import a recipe from a page you have open (in Safari with the extension, or in the in-app browser) | the address of the recipe's photo, if the page gives one | a request for the photo |
| share a link to Recimama, or use **From a video link** | the site of the link; then, if that page has no recipe, up to three recipe links found in its caption (or anywhere on the page, if it has no caption) | a request for those pages. No photo is fetched. |
| use **What's in my fridge**, **Recreate a dish** or **Photograph a menu** | DuckDuckGo, then up to three recipe pages from its results | the dish name you confirmed, or up to five ingredient names you confirmed, plus the word "recipe". The photo itself is never sent. |

Like any web request, these reveal your device's IP address and ordinary technical details to the site. The shared-link, video-link and "invent" requests identify the app as Safari on a Mac, and most of them ask for English pages, because many sites won't serve a page otherwise. The requests carry no identifier of ours, and we never see them. Each site handles what it receives under its own privacy policy. The in-app browser keeps no cookies or history once you close it. Sites contacted by the other requests can set cookies, which stay inside Recimama on your device, and we never read them.

## 8. What happens on your device

- **Dictation** is converted to text by Apple's speech recogniser, which the app forces to run on your device. If your device can't transcribe on-device, the app tells you rather than sending your voice to a server.
- **Photos you take or choose** are read on your device, using Apple's Vision framework to find text and recognise food. Photos and recordings never leave your device, except a card photo you choose to publish (section 3) or send (section 6).
- **Apple Intelligence**, where available, runs Apple's on-device model to read recipes and to write the "Invent something" ones.
- **Translating** a recipe uses Apple's Translation framework on your device. The first time you use a language, your device may download it from Apple.

## 9. Permissions

Recimama asks for each permission when you first use the feature that needs it:

- **Camera:** to photograph a cookbook page, a menu, a finished dish or your fridge, or to add a photo to a card.
- **Photos:** you pick photos with Apple's photo picker, which gives the app only the photo you choose. Saving a card image to Photos asks for permission to add to your library.
- **Microphone and Speech Recognition:** to dictate a recipe (see section 8).
- **Reminders:** to put a shopping list into a Reminders list you choose. The app reads the names of your lists so you can pick one, and adds items to it. Reminders syncs through your iCloud as usual.
- **Notifications:** step alerts while you cook; a weekly "Pantry check" reminder, which is on by default once your pantry has something in it (turn it off with **Weekly reminder** in Settings or on the Pantry screen); reminders about a rota day you signed up for; and a notice when someone on a rota calls off their meal. All of these are created on your device. We never send marketing notifications.

You can change any permission in the iPhone's **Settings > Apps > Recimama**.

## 10. No accounts, no analytics, no ads, no tracking

Recimama has no sign-up or login and no account with us. It contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or other third-party code. It doesn't use the advertising identifier and doesn't track you across other companies' apps or websites. It doesn't sell or share your information with anyone. If you've chosen to share crash reports with app developers in the iPhone's settings, Apple may pass us crash logs under Apple's own privacy policy.

## 11. Children

Because it has a web browser, Recimama is rated 16+ on the App Store in most places (15+ in Korea, and 17+ on devices using a system older than version 26, shown as 18+ in France). It isn't directed at children. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has published something to Explore, email [rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com) and we'll remove it.

## 13. Your rights

You can ask us what we hold about you, and ask us to correct or delete it, by emailing [rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com). The only information we can link to you is what you published or sent us: recipes, reports and emails. Because we don't know who you are from your iCloud identifier, tell us which recipes are yours (or send the request from the device you published from), so we can find them. You can withdraw consent at any time: take down what you published, stop sharing a rota, turn off iCloud sync (which stops your library being copied to iCloud and new rotas being put there automatically; a rota you already shared stays shared until you stop sharing it, as section 2 explains), or turn off a permission. Depending on where you live, you may also be able to complain to your data-protection authority.

Where the law asks us to state a legal basis: we publish your recipe because you asked us to, and we handle reports and blocks because keeping Explore safe is in the legitimate interest of everyone who uses it.
