# Recimama Privacy Policy

*Last updated: 29 September 2026*

Recimama is a cooking app made by David Rodriguez ("we", "us"), an individual developer in the United States. This policy describes what the app does with your information: what stays on your device, what goes to your own iCloud, what becomes public if you publish a recipe, what Apple and Google receive, and what other websites receive when you ask the app to fetch something.

**In short:** Recimama has no accounts. Your recipes stay on your device and in your own private iCloud, which we cannot see. We can see something about you in only two situations: when you **publish a recipe to Explore**, which makes it public, and when you **report something or email us**. The free version shows a few **ads from Google** while you browse recipes, never while you cook. Section 11 says exactly what Google receives, what it doesn't, and who never sees an ad. Google's ad software is the only third-party code in the app. We have no analytics of our own, we never ask to track you, and we never sell your information.

If you subscribe to **Recimama Pro**, Apple handles the purchase and the payment. The app stores no payment details and we never see them (section 10).

## 1. What is kept on your device

Recimama keeps these on your iPhone, iPad or Mac. If you're signed in to iCloud, they are also copied to your own private iCloud, unless you turn sync off (section 2):

- your recipes, including their photos, notes, ratings and tags, and your recipe collections
- what the app notes about how each recipe was added: for example, whether it was typed, pasted, dictated, invented or imported from a website, whether pasted text carried signs of having been copied (section 3), and whether its photo, when you chose it, carried a camera's details (section 8)
- your meal plans, pantry and saved menus (shopping lists are worked out from your plans)
- your cooking history (for example, when you cooked a recipe and how long it actually took)
- your meal rotas and dinner parties, including the sign-ups on them (section 5)

These stay on this device only:

- which shopping-list items you've ticked off
- the list of Explore recipes and cooks you have hidden or blocked
- your agreement to the community guidelines, and which version you agreed to
- the name you last typed when publishing, so you don't have to type it again
- your answer to the ads consent form, where one is shown (section 11), which Google's consent software keeps on the device

The app's widgets, its Siri shortcuts and its Share and Safari extensions read a small summary of this (what's cooking, what's planned for today, recipes you've clipped). That summary lives in a storage area shared only by Recimama's own parts on your device. If you have a paired Apple Watch, the step you're on and the steps coming next are sent straight to the watch while you cook, through Apple's WatchConnectivity.

## 2. Your private iCloud

**Settings > Storage > Sync with iCloud** is on unless you turn it off. While it's on and you're signed in to iCloud, everything in the first list in section 1 (your recipes and collections, plans, pantry, saved menus, cooking history, and your rotas and their sign-ups) is copied to your **private iCloud database** so it appears on your other devices. That database belongs to your Apple Account. We can't read it, and nobody else can unless you share a rota from it (section 5). Apple stores it under Apple's own terms and privacy policy.

Turning the switch off stops this copying from the next time you open the app, and straight away stops new rotas being put in iCloud automatically (section 5). What is already in iCloud stays there until you delete it (section 14). A rota you have already shared stays shared, so the people on it aren't cut off, until you stop sharing it. If you tap **Share > Invite people > Invite with a link** while the switch is off, that one rota is put in iCloud, because that is what the button is for. The switch doesn't affect publishing to Explore (section 3).

## 3. Explore: what becomes public when you publish

Explore is where people share recipes with everyone who uses Recimama. **Nothing is published unless you choose to.** For each recipe you publish, you open its menu, choose **Share with everyone**, and tap **Share this recipe**. Before your first share, and again whenever the community guidelines change, you agree to them. **Every time** you share, you also confirm with a switch that you wrote the recipe yourself (or that Recimama invented it for you), that you took its photo, and that it isn't copied from a website, book, app, video or another person. Publishing needs you to be signed in to iCloud; browsing doesn't.

**What is published.** The recipe is saved in Recimama's **public iCloud database**, which Apple hosts for us (section 12). The record contains:

- the recipe's title, description, servings, ingredients (as written, with any notes), method steps and each step's timing and oven temperature, tags, emoji and card colour
- its total and hands-on time and how many steps it has
- **the card's photo**, if the recipe has one and it isn't a picture downloaded from a website. If the app can't tell that a camera took the photo, it first asks whether you took it. That happens when the photo, as you chose it, carried no camera details, or was exactly the size of a screen, as a screenshot is, and for a photo added before the app kept track of this (section 8). The app redraws every photo it publishes, which removes the location and camera details that phones store inside photos.
- **a fingerprint of that photo**: a short code worked out from it, from which the photo can't be recreated. It lets us find identical copies of a photo reported as an intimate image (section 4).
- **your name**, if "Your name (optional)" has one in it when you tap Share this recipe (up to 40 characters). After your first publish, that box starts with the name you used last time, so clear it if you don't want your name on this recipe. If it's empty, the recipe shows as "Someone".
- **your note**, only if you type one in "One line about it (optional)" (up to 200 characters)
- **a label**, if Recimama invented the recipe with Apple Intelligence: "Invented with Apple Intelligence", shown with a reminder to check the food
- **which version of the community guidelines you agreed to**
- the date you published it, and small technical details the app uses to read the recipe back (for example, how the recipe was made, such as typed, pasted, dictated or invented, and a format version)
- **an identifier for your iCloud account.** iCloud adds this to every public record automatically. It is a random code that iCloud creates for your Apple Account within Recimama. It doesn't contain your name, email address or phone number. It is the same on every recipe you publish, though, so it links them together. Recimama uses it so people can block a cook and so we can act on reports.

**Who can see it.** Anyone using Recimama can see your published recipe, including people who aren't signed in to iCloud, and anyone can add a copy to their own library. We can also see everything in the public database, including the iCloud identifier. What you give us permission to do with a published recipe is in the [Terms of Use](terms.html).

**What you can't publish.** Only recipes you wrote yourself, or that Recimama invented for you with Apple Intelligence, can be published. The app refuses:

- recipes imported from websites, read from a photographed page, that came from Explore or from another person, or that came with Recimama
- recipes written by following a recipe published on a website
- text you pasted or shared into the app, if it contains a web address, ©, "All rights reserved", "Jump to recipe", "Print recipe", "Nutrition Facts" or "mealime". Those are signs of text copied from a website or another app, and the app says which one it found.
- anything with a word Explore doesn't allow
- anything from a cook who has been blocked from Explore (section 4)

All of these checks happen on your device, and nothing is sent when a recipe is refused.

**Taking it down.** In the recipe's **More** menu choose **Shared — manage it**, or go to **Explore > More > Recipes you shared**. Swipe left on the recipe, tap **Take down**, then confirm with **Take it down**. The recipe stops being shown on Explore for everyone, normally within moments, though a list somebody already has open keeps it until Explore loads again. To find recipes you shared from another of your devices, use **Settings > About > Remove something I published**. Copies that people have already added to their own libraries stay with them. We can't reach those, and neither can you.

**If you can't take it down yourself** (for example, you have a new phone), email **[rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com)** with the recipe's title and roughly when you published it. We aim to delete it within 24 hours of reading your email. If you'd like us to remove *everything* you've published, tell us, and we'll delete all the public records carrying your iCloud identifier.

**If something on Explore is yours or is about you.** If somebody else shared your recipe or your photo, see our [Copyright page](copyright.html). If a recipe shows you or gives out your personal information, tap **Report** on it (section 4), or email us. To report anything illegal, with or without the app, see [Report illegal content](report.html).

## 4. Reports, blocks and moderation

**Reporting.** Every Explore recipe has a **Report** button. A report records which recipe you reported, the reason you chose, any comment you add (up to 200 characters), the time, and the identifier of the cook who published it. The reasons are: offensive or hateful; sexual content; spam, or not a recipe; copied from somebody else; illegal content; an intimate image of you shared without your consent; and something else. If you choose illegal content, the report also records the law you name and what you say is illegal. The report is saved to the public iCloud database, where only we can read it. Other users can't, apart from your own iCloud account, which filed it. iCloud also attaches **your** iCloud identifier to it, as it does to anything saved there.

**If you'd like a reply, or the report can't be sent** (for example, you're offline), the app offers to write the report as an email to [rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com), with the recipe and your reason filled in. That email comes from your own mail account, so we'll see your email address and anything you add. **Your email address reaches us only in that email.** The app never saves it with the report, in iCloud or anywhere else. We use these details only to deal with the report and to reply to you.

**An intimate image reported by the person in it.** Every photo published on Explore carries its fingerprint (section 3). When someone reports an intimate image of themselves, we use the reported photo's fingerprint to look for other recipes with exactly the same photo. Before treating one as a copy, the moderator's copy of the app works out its fingerprint again from the photo itself, and a person decides what to take down.

**What we do with reports.** A person reads every report, and we aim to review reports within 24 hours. An intimate image shared without consent is taken down, with any identical copies we find, within 48 hours of the report at most. If a recipe breaks the [community guidelines](guidelines.html) or the law, we delete it. If a cook breaks them seriously or repeatedly, we add their iCloud identifier to a **blocked list**. The app then hides their recipes for everyone and won't let them publish again. That includes a cook named in two valid copyright notices, whose shared recipes are also all removed (see the [Copyright page](copyright.html)). The blocked list holds only iCloud identifiers and the date each was added: no names, recipes or reasons. **Every copy of Recimama can read it**, because each copy has to know whose recipes to hide, so anyone who looks can see that an identifier is blocked, but not whose it is or why.

**Our moderation log.** Every removal, block, dismissal or preservation we make in the app writes a line to a log that only we can read. It records:

- what we did and on what basis (for example: copyright, illegal content, an intimate image, child sexual abuse material, or the guidelines), and for a copyright notice, which notice it was
- the recipe's record name and its title at the time, except for child sexual abuse material, where the title is left out
- the reasons chosen and how many reports there were
- when the first report arrived and when we acted
- for a removal or a block, the cook's iCloud identifier and the fingerprint of the recipe's photo, if it had one

It never holds what a reporter wrote or who they were. We keep it to account for our decisions, to answer an appeal or a counter-notice, and to count repeated infringement. Section 14 says for how long.

**Child sexual abuse material.** If a listing appears to contain it, we take the listing down and report it to the US National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), as US law requires. The same law asks us to preserve what we reported for one year. So a copy of that listing, with the reports about it (their reasons, comments, any law they name and their dates, never who filed them), is kept **only on the moderator's own device**: protected by the device's encryption, left out of backups, never synced to iCloud and never included in any export. It is deleted automatically a year and 30 days after it was kept, so that the year runs from our report to NCMEC, which we make straight away. It is never written to Explore's public database.

**Other people we may tell.** If something suggests a threat to someone's life or safety, we may tell the police or other authorities. If you send a copyright counter-notice, we forward it, with your name and address, to the person who sent the notice, as US law requires. If a cook asks why their recipe was taken down after a copyright notice, we may give them a copy of that notice, including the name of whoever sent it, but not their address, phone number or email address. We don't tell a cook who reported them for anything else.

**Blocking a cook.** **Block** hides everything that cook publishes on your device, and also sends us a report saying you blocked them, so we can spot someone many people are blocking. **Hide this** hides one recipe on your device and tells nobody. Your blocked and hidden lists stay on your device. You can undo them from Explore's **More** menu.

## 5. Meal rotas and dinner parties

A meal rota (people taking turns to bring meals) or a dinner party ("A meal with friends") reaches the people you send it to, and anyone they pass it on to. We never see any of it.

- **Live sharing (needs iCloud).** If you're signed in to iCloud and **Sync with iCloud** is on, the app puts every rota you organise into your private iCloud database and creates a sharing link for it: when you first save the rota, and when you open a rota you made earlier. With the switch off it does this only for a rota you ask to share with **Invite with a link** (section 2). Nobody can open the rota until the link leaves your device. It leaves in three ways: **Share > Send the link**; **Share > Invite people > Invite with a link** (called **Add someone to it** once the rota is live); and any invitation message you send or copy while the rota is live (next point). **Anyone who has the link can open the rota and sign up**, so send it only to people you mean to invite, and remember it can be forwarded. Everyone on the rota can see what you entered: who it's for, the dates, how many each meal feeds, what the household can't eat, and your notes and requests. They can also see every sign-up: each person's name, dish and note, and the dish's ingredient names, which are used to check it against what the household can't eat. **Share > Manage who's on it** opens Apple's sharing screen, where the organiser can see and remove the people who joined, and stop sharing. It may show participants' Apple Account names. Once you stop sharing, the app doesn't share that rota again unless you invite people to it again.
- **An invitation message** (Share > Invite people, then **Send the invitation** or **Copy the message**) says who the meals are for, the open days or courses, how many each meal feeds, what the household can't eat, anything else you've asked people to bring, and your note. Unless the rota has finished, it ends with a link:
  - **If the rota is live, the link is its sharing link.** Anyone who gets the message, or has it forwarded to them, can open the whole rota, including everyone's names and sign-ups, and can sign up themselves.
  - Otherwise, it's a Recimama link that carries the rota's title, note, dates, servings, dietary needs, courses and requests, but no sign-ups.
- **A rota file** (Share > Send the rota) contains the whole rota, including everyone's sign-ups.
- **Copy as a message** is plain text you paste wherever you like. It gives the rota's name, what the household can eat, how many it feeds and your note, then each day with the name and dish of whoever signed up for it.

These go wherever you send them (Messages, Mail, AirDrop and so on), and the recipients' apps handle them under their own terms.

## 6. Recipe cards and backups you send

**Share recipe**, **Make a card** and **Settings > Backup > Export a backup** create files: a recipe file, an image or PDF, or a backup of your whole library. They go only where you send or save them, and we never receive them.

## 7. When the app goes online for you

Apart from iCloud, Apple's App Store (section 10) and the ads (section 11, the one thing the app fetches without being asked), the app connects to other websites only when you ask it to, and only to do what you asked:

| When you… | The app contacts… | What it sends |
|---|---|---|
| use **Find one on the web** (the in-app browser) | DuckDuckGo if you type words (with "recipe" added unless you typed it), or the site you type or tap | your search or the address, as any browser would |
| import a recipe from a page you have open (in Safari with the extension, or in the in-app browser) | the address of the recipe's photo, if the page gives one, except on an Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or Threads page, or a photo on their servers | a request for the photo |
| share a link to Recimama, use **From a video link**, or paste recipe links into **Paste text** or **Bring recipes from another app** | the site of each link, one at a time, **except Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and Threads, which the app never contacts** — not for the page, not for a link that redirects to one of them, and not for a photo. Then, if that page has no recipe, it contacts up to three recipe links found in its caption (or anywhere on the page, if it has no caption) | a request for those pages. No photo or video is fetched. |
| use **What's in my fridge**, **Recreate a dish** or **Photograph a menu** | DuckDuckGo, then up to three recipe pages from its results | the dish name you confirmed, or up to five ingredient names you confirmed, plus the word "recipe". The photo itself is never sent. |

For a post on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or Threads, share the post to Recimama from that app, or copy its caption into **Paste text**. A shared post brings its caption text with it, and the app reads that text without contacting anyone. No video is downloaded.

Like any web request, these reveal your device's IP address and ordinary technical details to the site. The shared-link, video-link and "invent" requests identify the app as Safari on a Mac (they never go to Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or Threads), and most of them ask for English pages, because many sites won't serve a page otherwise. The requests carry no identifier of ours, and we never see them. Each site handles what it receives under its own privacy policy. The in-app browser keeps no cookies or history once you close it. The app's other requests all go through one private connection that neither sends nor accepts cookies and saves nothing to your device's storage.

## 8. What happens on your device

- **Dictation** is converted to text by Apple's speech recogniser, which the app forces to run on your device. If your device can't transcribe on-device, the app tells you rather than sending your voice to a server.
- **Photos you take or choose** are read on your device, using Apple's Vision framework to find text and recognise food. When you choose a photo for a recipe, the app also reads, on your device, whether the file carries a camera's details and whether it is exactly the size of a screen. It keeps only a one-word note of the answer with the recipe (section 1), not the details themselves, and uses it to ask whether you took the photo before it can be published (section 3). Photos and recordings never leave your device, except a card photo you choose to publish (section 3) or send (section 6).
- **PDFs you choose** are read on your device: their text by Apple's PDFKit, and scanned pages by the same Vision reading as a photo. A PDF never leaves your device.
- **Apple Intelligence**, where available, runs Apple's on-device model to read recipes and to write the "Invent something" ones.
- **Translating** a recipe uses Apple's Translation framework on your device. The first time you use a language, your device may download it from Apple.
- **Explore's checks** (the word filter, the copying checks, the photo question and the block-list check, section 3) all run on your device.

## 9. Permissions

Recimama asks for each permission when you first use the feature that needs it:

- **Camera:** to photograph a cookbook page, a menu, a finished dish or your fridge, or to add a photo to a card.
- **Photos:** you pick photos with Apple's photo picker, which gives the app only the photo you choose. Saving a card image to Photos asks for permission to add to your library.
- **Microphone and Speech Recognition:** to dictate a recipe (see section 8).
- **Reminders:** to put a shopping list into a Reminders list you choose. The app reads the names of your lists so you can pick one, and adds items to it. Reminders syncs through your iCloud as usual.
- **Notifications:** step alerts while you cook; a weekly "Pantry check" reminder, which is on by default once your pantry has something in it (turn it off with **Weekly reminder** in Settings or on the Pantry screen); reminders about a rota day you signed up for; and a notice when someone on a rota calls off their meal. All of these are created on your device. We never send marketing notifications.

You can change any permission in the iPhone's or iPad's **Settings > Apps > Recimama**.

## 10. Recimama Pro: purchases are handled by Apple

Recimama Pro is an optional subscription, sold through the App Store, that adds five planning features. Everything else in Recimama is free.

- **Apple takes the payment.** You buy, pay for, change and cancel Recimama Pro with your Apple Account, under Apple's terms and Apple's privacy policy. Apple processes the purchase. Recimama never receives or stores your card, billing address, name or email address, and there is no Recimama account to sign in to.
- **What the app checks, on your device.** To know whether Pro is on, the app asks StoreKit, Apple's purchasing system on your device. StoreKit says which plan you have, whether it is in its free trial or shared with you by your family, whether you have ever paid for it, and when the current period ends. The app asks when you open it, when you come back to it, and when a purchase changes. The answer is used on your device: to unlock the features, to fill in **Settings > Purchases**, and to decide whether you see ads (section 11). The app sends none of it to us, and keeps no record of it beyond what StoreKit itself keeps.
- **What we can see.** Apple gives developers sales and subscription reports: for example, how many people started a trial or renewed, by country. They don't tell us who you are.
- **Family Sharing.** Recimama Pro can be shared with your family group. If someone in your family subscribes, Apple tells the app on your device only that Pro is shared with you.
- **Cancelling and refunds.** On an iPhone or iPad, cancel in **Settings > [your name] > Subscriptions**. On a Mac, cancel in the **App Store > [your name] > Subscriptions**. Or, on any of them, use **Settings > Purchases > Manage Subscription** in Recimama. Cancel at least 24 hours before the end of the current period, or of a free trial, or you'll be charged for the next one. If the price ever changes, Apple tells you first. Refunds are requested from Apple, at [reportaproblem.apple.com](https://reportaproblem.apple.com). If Pro ends, nothing you made with it is deleted.

## 11. Ads

**Where they are.** A few ads appear while you browse:

- among your own recipes in the Recipes tab
- among shared recipes in Explore
- as one result when you search your library
- as one banner at the bottom of the meal plan

There are **no ads anywhere else**: not while you're cooking, not on a recipe, and not in the shopping list, the shared rotas, the import screens, Settings, the widgets, the notifications, the Apple Watch app or the Safari and share extensions. While the Recimama Pro screen is open, the ads under it are hidden. This version of Recimama has **no full-screen (interstitial), app-open, video or rewarded ads**, and an ad never interrupts what you're doing. If a later version adds another kind of ad, this policy will say so before that version is released.

**Who never sees ads.**

- **Anyone who has ever paid for Recimama Pro**, even after the subscription has ended. A payment Apple refunded doesn't count.
- **Anyone in a free trial of Recimama Pro, or getting Pro through Family Sharing**, while the trial or the sharing lasts. If you've never paid yourself, the ads come back when the trial or the sharing ends.
- **Anyone using Recimama on a Mac.** Google doesn't say that its ad software supports iPad apps running on a Mac, so Recimama doesn't start it there, and shows no ads on a Mac.

**When Google is contacted.** Each time Recimama opens, it first asks StoreKit (section 10) about your purchases. It doesn't contact Google, not even to ask for your consent, until StoreKit has answered. If you've paid, it never starts Google's software, so Google receives nothing from Recimama about you. If you pay while the app is open, it stops asking for ads straight away, and from the next time it opens Google's software isn't started at all.

**Who shows them.** Google, through the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob) and Google's consent software (the User Messaging Platform). Together they are the only third-party code in the app. **For everyone, we turn on Google's "restricted data processing"** ([Google's explanation](https://business.safety.google/rdp/)). Under US state privacy laws, this makes Google our service provider for the ads. It may use what it receives only for limited purposes, such as showing and counting ads, limiting how often you see the same one, and detecting fraud. It may not use it to build a profile of you or to personalise ads. Elsewhere, Google is responsible for what it receives as a controller in its own right, under its privacy policy and the choices you make in its consent form (below). **Google is bound by its contract with us (the AdMob terms and Google's data protection terms) to protect the information it receives, as this policy describes and as the law requires.** Google's privacy policy is at [policies.google.com/privacy](https://policies.google.com/privacy), and how Google uses information from apps that use its services is at [policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites](https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites).

**What Google receives.** To choose, show and count an ad, Google receives:

- your device's **IP address**, from which it estimates a rough location (for example, which city)
- a **device identifier** that Google's own SDK creates or reads
- **which ads you were shown and which you tapped**
- **technical information about the app and your device**: the app and SDK version, the device model and system version, and diagnostic data such as start-up time, crashes and energy use

**What Google does not receive.** Your recipes, their photos, your notes, ratings, tags and collections, your meal plans, pantry, shopping lists, cooking history, rotas and dinner parties, your dietary needs, your dictation, anything you photograph, and whether you've bought anything. None of it is sent to Google, and none of it is used to choose an ad.

**The ads are not personalised.** We ask Google for non-personalised ads only. They are chosen from where you are roughly, and from the fact that you're using a cooking app. They are not chosen from your past behaviour, your interests, or anything you've done in other apps or on the web. We also switch off Google's own "publisher first-party ID", and we cap ad content at "parental guidance" so an ad suits the app's rating.

**We don't track you.** The app never shows the "Allow app to track your activity?" prompt. So your device's advertising identifier stays hidden from it (iOS gives an app only zeros unless you've allowed tracking), and Google's SDK doesn't send it (Google: "The Google Mobile Ads SDK doesn't send IDFA in the ad request", [developers.google.com/admob/ios/privacy/idfa](https://developers.google.com/admob/ios/privacy/idfa)). The App Store listing therefore says no data is used to track you. The device identifier Google does receive is one of its own. Even non-personalised ads use an identifier for **frequency capping**, so you don't see the same ad over and over, and for counting how often an ad was shown ([support.google.com/admob/answer/7676680](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/7676680)). What Google does on its own servers is governed by Google's privacy policy, and we can't see into it.

**Consent.** If you're in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app shows Google's own consent form before it requests any ad, and no ad is requested while the form is waiting for your answer. Wherever you are, if Google's consent software can't say whether you need to be asked, the app requests no ads at all. You can change your answer at any time in **Settings > Ads > Ad privacy choices**, which reopens the same form. There you can refuse each use Google lists, including the uses Google bases on its legitimate interests. **Declining, or withdrawing consent later, doesn't stop the ads.** Google then shows you ads that aren't chosen from your personal data: non-personalised ads, or what it calls "limited ads", which also use no identifier stored on your device. It shows limited ads if you haven't agreed to Google storing information on your device ([support.google.com/admob/answer/13554116](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/13554116) and [support.google.com/admob/answer/10105530](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/10105530)).

**Having no ads, and what else you can change.**

- **Pay for Recimama Pro.** This is the one way to have no ads, and it lasts even if you later cancel. A free trial or Family Sharing also hides them, while it lasts.
- **Change your consent choice** (EEA, UK, Switzerland): Settings > Ads > Ad privacy choices. This changes what Google may do with your data, as above; the ads themselves carry on.
- **The iPhone's own ad settings don't apply to Recimama's ads.** *Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking* decides which apps may ask to track you, and Recimama never asks. *Settings > Privacy & Security > Apple Advertising* is about Apple's own ads in the App Store, Apple News and Stocks, not Google's.

**Test versions.** Test versions of Recimama, from TestFlight or run by the developer from Xcode, show Google's sample ads instead of real ones. So does a copy from the App Store on any launch where the app can't confirm how it was installed. Sample ads earn nothing, but Google receives the same information to show them.

**Reporting an ad.** Every ad carries an "Ad" label and a flag button. Tapping the flag writes us an email with that ad's reference, so we can identify it and block it in our AdMob account. Ads are sold by Google, not chosen by us, so reporting them is the only way we can act on one.

**Install measurement.** The app includes Apple's SKAdNetwork identifiers for Google and the ad buyers bidding through it. That is Apple's own system for telling an advertiser that an install followed an ad **without identifying you**. It needs no permission and sends nothing about you.

If you've chosen to share crash reports with app developers in your device's settings, Apple may pass us crash logs under Apple's own privacy policy.

## 15. Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us:

- what information we hold about you, and for a copy of it
- to correct it
- to delete it
- to stop or limit using it while a question about it is settled
- for the recipes you published, as a file you can use elsewhere

Email **[rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com)**. We'll answer within one month. If a request is complicated we may need up to two more months, and we'll tell you if so. It's free.

The only information we can link to you is what you published or sent us: recipes, reports and emails. We don't know who you are from your iCloud identifier. So tell us which recipes are yours, or send the request from the device you published from, so we can find them. A rota you already shared stays shared until you stop sharing it, as section 2 explains. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, you can change your ads choice in Settings > Ads > Ad privacy choices (section 11).

You won't be treated any differently for using any of these rights. If you're unhappy with how we've handled your information, tell us (section 18 says how we handle complaints). Depending on where you live, you may also be able to complain to your data-protection authority.

## 16. Your right to object

**You have the right to object**, at any time, on grounds relating to your particular situation, to our using your information where we rely on our legitimate interests. Section 18 lists where we do: checking a recipe before it's published, handling reports and blocks, keeping the blocked list and the moderation log, preserving material reported to NCMEC, handling copyright notices and counter-notices, answering emails, and showing ads where they aren't based on consent. To object, email **[rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com)** and say what you object to. We'll stop, unless we have compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests, rights and freedoms (for example, keeping a block on a cook who shared illegal content), or we need the information for legal claims.

For the ads, you can also object in **Settings > Ads > Ad privacy choices**, where that form is shown (section 11). It lets you refuse each use Google lists, including those Google bases on its legitimate interests.

We never use your information for direct marketing. If we ever did, you could object to that at any time, for any reason, and we would stop.

## 17. US state privacy laws, Do Not Track, and other companies

**We don't sell or share your personal information**, as California and other US states define those words, and we don't use it for targeted advertising. For everyone, we turn on Google's restricted data processing, so Google acts as our service provider for the ads (section 11). We have no actual knowledge of selling or sharing the personal information of anyone under 16. We don't disclose personal information to anyone for their own direct marketing.

**Your rights under those laws** are the ones in section 15: to know what we hold, to get a copy, to correct it and to delete it. Anyone can use them, wherever they live. **Do Not Track.** Recimama doesn't track you across other companies' apps and websites, so there is nothing for a browser's "Do Not Track" signal to switch off, and the app doesn't change what it does when it receives one.

**Other companies collecting information through Recimama.** Google is the only company that collects information through Recimama about how you use it: which ads you were shown and tapped, and a device identifier of its own (section 11). It can use that over time, for example to stop showing you the same ad. We don't let it be used to personalise ads to you, and Recimama lets no company track you across other companies' apps and websites. Websites you ask the app to reach (section 7) receive what any website receives from a visitor.
