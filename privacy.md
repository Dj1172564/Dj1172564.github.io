# Recimama Privacy Policy

*Last updated: 19 October 2026*

Recimama is a cooking app made by David Rodriguez ("we", "us"), an individual developer in the United States. This policy describes what the app does with your information: what stays on your device, what goes to your own iCloud, what becomes public if you publish a recipe, what Apple and Google receive, and what other websites receive when you ask the app to fetch something.

**In short:** Recimama has no accounts. Your recipes stay on your device and in your own private iCloud, which we cannot see. We can see something about you in only two situations: when you **publish a recipe to Explore**, which makes it public, and when you **report something or email us**. The free version shows a few **ads from Google** while you browse recipes, never while you cook. Section 11 says exactly what Google receives, what it doesn't, and who never sees an ad. Google's ad software is the only third-party code in the app. We have no analytics of our own, we never ask to track you, and we never sell your information.

If you buy **Recimama Pro**, as a subscription or as Lifetime, Apple handles the purchase and the payment. The app stores no payment details and we never see them (section 10).

## 1. What is kept on your device

Recimama keeps these on your iPhone, iPad, Mac or Apple Vision Pro. If you're signed in to iCloud, they are also copied to your own private iCloud, unless you turn sync off (section 2):

- your recipes, including their photos, notes, ratings and tags, and your recipe collections
- what the app notes about how each recipe was added: for example, whether it was typed, pasted, dictated, invented or imported from a website, whether pasted text carried signs of having been copied (section 3), and whether its photo, when you chose it, carried a camera's details (section 8), and, when steps were added to it by **Watch the video for the steps** or from a similar recipe (sections 7 and 8), a line in its description saying so (for a similar recipe, with its title, site and address)
- your meal plans, pantry and saved menus (shopping lists are worked out from your plans)
- which dish you've said goes into another when you plan a meal, and at which step (for example, that your tomato sauce goes into the shakshuka), which dishes you've said to finish before others start (for example, the rice before the stir-fry), and the ones you've said to serve on their own or cook in any order
- your cooking history (for example, when you cooked a recipe and how long it actually took)
- whether you've turned on **Move on when a step's time is up** (Settings > While cooking), so every device you cook on does the same
- your meal trains and dinner parties, including the sign-ups on them (section 5)
- whether you've hidden the sample recipes Recimama started your library with, which of those samples your library has already been given (so a new device doesn't add them a second time), and which samples the app has moved to Recently Deleted (so one you bring back stays)

These stay on this device only:

- which shopping-list items you've ticked off
- the list of Explore recipes and cooks you have hidden or blocked
- your agreement to the community guidelines, and which version you agreed to
- the name you last typed when publishing, so you don't have to type it again
- which search engine Recimama's browser searches with: DuckDuckGo, unless you pick Google, Bing or Brave Search on its toolbar
- which recipes you've told the duplicate finder are different recipes ("Keep both"), and whether you've put away its note at the top of your library
- your answer to the ads consent form, where one is shown (section 11), which Google's consent software keeps on the device
- when you first opened the app and, if you don't have Recimama Pro, when the app last suggested Pro to you after a cook, how many suggestions there have been, and how many times in a row you've answered "Not now", so that those suggestions stay rare (section 10)
- how many cooks you've finished on this device, and when (and in which version of the app) it last asked you to rate it on the App Store, so that it asks rarely: at most once a version, and never before your third finished cook

The app's widgets, its Siri shortcuts and its Share and Safari extensions read a small summary of this (what's cooking, what's planned for today, recipes you've clipped). That summary lives in a storage area shared only by Recimama's own parts on your device. A clip you haven't imported yet is kept there too, with what the app read from it (the recipe's text, its photo and the name of the account that posted it, and, for an Instagram post whose caption has the ingredients but not the steps, where Instagram's page said its public video is, how long it runs and how long that address works), until you import it or discard it. A **video you share to Recimama** is copied there as well, so the app can read it (section 8); the copy is deleted as soon as the app has read it, and within a day even if you never open it. None of this ever leaves your device. If you have a paired Apple Watch, the step you're on, the steps coming next, your timers and what's on the back burner are sent straight to the watch while you cook, through Apple's WatchConnectivity.

## 2. Your private iCloud

**Settings > Storage > Sync with iCloud** is on unless you turn it off. While it's on and you're signed in to iCloud, everything in the first list in section 1 (your recipes and collections, plans, pantry, saved menus, cooking history, and your meal trains and their sign-ups) is copied to your **private iCloud database** so it appears on your other devices. That database belongs to your Apple Account. We can't read it, and nobody else can unless you share a meal train from it (section 5). Apple stores it under Apple's own terms and privacy policy. The few settings about the sample recipes in that list, and which dish goes into another and which you finish before another, go to your iCloud's **key-value storage** instead, which is just as private: only your own devices signed in to your Apple Account can read it, we can't read it either, and the same switch turns it off. A choice about two dishes is removed from it when either recipe is deleted for good.

Turning the switch off stops this copying from the next time you open the app, and straight away stops new meal trains being put in iCloud automatically (section 5). What is already in iCloud stays there until you delete it (section 14). A meal train you have already shared stays shared, so the people on it aren't cut off, until you stop sharing it. If you tap **Share > Invite people > Invite with a link** while the switch is off, that one meal train is put in iCloud, because that is what the button is for. The switch doesn't affect publishing to Explore (section 3).

## 3. Explore: what becomes public when you publish

Explore is where people share recipes with everyone who uses Recimama. **Nothing is published unless you choose to.** For each recipe you publish, you open its menu, choose **Share with everyone**, and tap **Share this recipe**. Before your first share, and again whenever the community guidelines change, you agree to them. **Every time** you share, you also confirm with a switch that you wrote the recipe yourself (or that Recimama invented it for you), that you took its photo, and that it isn't copied from a website, book, app, video or another person. Publishing needs you to be signed in to iCloud; browsing doesn't.

**What is published.** The recipe is saved in Recimama's **public iCloud database**, which Apple hosts for us (section 12). The record contains:

- the recipe's title, description, servings, ingredients (as written, with any notes), method steps and each step's timing and oven temperature, tags, emoji and card colour
- its total and hands-on time and how many steps it has
- **the card's photo**, if the recipe has one and it isn't a picture downloaded from a website. If the app can't tell that a camera took the photo, it first asks whether you took it. That happens when the photo, as you chose it, carried no camera details, or was exactly the size of a screen, as a screenshot is, and for a photo added before the app kept track of this (section 8). The app redraws every photo it publishes, which removes the location and camera details that phones store inside photos.
- **a fingerprint of that photo**: a short code worked out from it, from which the photo can't be recreated. It lets us find identical copies of a photo reported as an intimate image (section 4).
- **your name**, if "Your name (optional)" has one in it when you tap Share this recipe (up to 40 characters). After your first publish, that box starts with the name you used last time, so clear it if you don't want your name on this recipe. If it's empty, the recipe shows as "Someone".
- **your note**, only if you type one in "One line about it (optional)" (up to 200 characters)
- **a label**, if Recimama invented the recipe with Apple Intelligence: "Invented with Apple Intelligence", shown with a reminder to check the food
- **which version of the community guidelines you agreed to**
- the date you published it, and small technical details the app uses to read the recipe back (for example, how the recipe was made, such as typed, pasted, dictated or invented, and a format version)
- **an identifier for your iCloud account.** iCloud adds this to every public record automatically. It is a random code that iCloud creates for your Apple Account within Recimama. It doesn't contain your name, email address or phone number. It is the same on every recipe you publish, though, so it links them together. Recimama uses it so people can block a cook and so we can act on reports.

**Who can see it.** Anyone using Recimama can see your published recipe, including people who aren't signed in to iCloud, and anyone can add a copy to their own library. We can also see everything in the public database, including the iCloud identifier. What you give us permission to do with a published recipe is in the [Terms of Use](terms.html).

**What you can't publish.** Only recipes you wrote yourself, or that Recimama invented for you with Apple Intelligence, can be published. The app refuses:

- recipes imported from websites, read from a photographed page, that came from Explore or from another person, or that came with Recimama
- recipes written by following a recipe published on a website
- text you pasted or shared into the app, if it contains a web address, ©, "All rights reserved", "Jump to recipe", "Print recipe", "Nutrition Facts" or "mealime". Those are signs of text copied from a website or another app, and the app says which one it found.
- anything with a word Explore doesn't allow
- anything from a cook who has been blocked from Explore (section 4)

All of these checks happen on your device, and nothing is sent when a recipe is refused.

**Taking it down.** Open the recipe and choose **Remove from Explore** (it's beside **On Explore** on the recipe, and in its **More** menu), or open your recipe on Explore and remove it there. **Settings > Your recipes on Explore** lists every recipe you've published from any of your devices, even one you've since deleted from your library, with **Remove** on each. The app asks first, then deletes the published recipe. It stops being shown on Explore for everyone, normally within moments, though a list somebody already has open keeps it until Explore loads again. Your own copy stays in your library. Copies that people have already added to their own libraries stay with them. We can't reach those, and neither can you.

**Removing everything you've published.** In **Settings > Your recipes on Explore**, tap **Remove all from Explore**. It deletes every public recipe record your iCloud account created.

**If you can't take it down yourself** (for example, you published it while signed in to a different Apple Account), email **[rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com)** with the recipe's title and roughly when you published it. We aim to delete it within 24 hours of reading your email. If you'd like us to remove *everything* you've published, tell us, and we'll delete all the public records carrying your iCloud identifier.

**If something on Explore is yours or is about you.** If somebody else shared your recipe or your photo, see our [Copyright page](copyright.html). If a recipe shows you or gives out your personal information, tap **Report** on it (section 4), or email us. To report anything illegal, with or without the app, see [Report illegal content](report.html).

## 4. Reports, blocks and moderation

**Reporting.** Every Explore recipe has a **Report** button. A report records which recipe you reported, the reason you chose, any comment you add (up to 200 characters), the time, and the identifier of the cook who published it. The reasons are: offensive or hateful; sexual content; spam, or not a recipe; copied from somebody else; illegal content; an intimate image of you shared without your consent; and something else. If you choose illegal content, the report also records the law you name and what you say is illegal. The report is saved to the public iCloud database, where only we can read it. Other users can't, apart from your own iCloud account, which filed it. iCloud also attaches **your** iCloud identifier to it, as it does to anything saved there.

**If you'd like a reply, or the report can't be sent** (for example, you're offline), the app offers to write the report as an email to [rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com), with the recipe and your reason filled in. That email comes from your own mail account, so we'll see your email address and anything you add. **Your email address reaches us only in that email.** The app never saves it with the report, in iCloud or anywhere else. We use these details only to deal with the report and to reply to you.

**An intimate image reported by the person in it.** Every photo published on Explore carries its fingerprint (section 3). When someone reports an intimate image of themselves, we use the reported photo's fingerprint to look for other recipes with exactly the same photo. Before treating one as a copy, the moderator's copy of the app works out its fingerprint again from the photo itself, and a person decides what to take down.

**What we do with reports.** A person reads every report, and we aim to review reports within 24 hours. An intimate image shared without consent is taken down, with any identical copies we find, within 48 hours of the report at most. If a recipe breaks the [community guidelines](guidelines.html) or the law, we delete it. If a cook breaks them seriously or repeatedly, we add their iCloud identifier to a **blocked list**. The app then hides their recipes for everyone and won't let them publish again. That includes a cook named in two valid copyright notices, whose shared recipes are also all removed (see the [Copyright page](copyright.html)). The blocked list holds only iCloud identifiers and the date each was added: no names, recipes or reasons. **Every copy of Recimama can read it**, because each copy has to know whose recipes to hide, so anyone who looks can see that an identifier is blocked, but not whose it is or why.

**Our moderation log.** Every removal, block, dismissal or preservation we make in the app writes a line to a log that only we can read. It records:

- what we did and on what basis (for example: copyright, illegal content, an intimate image, child sexual abuse material, or the guidelines), and for a copyright notice, which notice it was
- the recipe's record name and its title at the time, except for child sexual abuse material, where the title is left out
- the reasons chosen and how many reports there were
- when the first report arrived and when we acted
- for a removal or a block, the cook's iCloud identifier and the fingerprint of the recipe's photo, if it had one

It never holds what a reporter wrote or who they were. We keep it to account for our decisions, to answer an appeal or a counter-notice, and to count repeated infringement. Section 14 says for how long.

**Child sexual abuse material.** If a listing appears to contain it, we take the listing down and report it to the US National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC), as US law requires. The same law asks us to preserve what we reported for one year. So a copy of that listing, with the reports about it (their reasons, comments, any law they name and their dates, never who filed them), is kept **only on the moderator's own device**: protected by the device's encryption, left out of backups, never synced to iCloud and never included in any export. It is deleted automatically a year and 30 days after it was kept, so that the year runs from our report to NCMEC, which we make straight away. It is never written to Explore's public database.

**Other people we may tell.** If something suggests a threat to someone's life or safety, we may tell the police or other authorities. If you send a copyright counter-notice, we forward it, with your name and address, to the person who sent the notice, as US law requires. If a cook asks why their recipe was taken down after a copyright notice, we may give them a copy of that notice, including the name of whoever sent it, but not their address, phone number or email address. We don't tell a cook who reported them for anything else.

**Blocking a cook.** **Block** hides everything that cook publishes on your device, and also sends us a report saying you blocked them, so we can spot someone many people are blocking. **Hide this** hides one recipe on your device and tells nobody. Your blocked and hidden lists stay on your device. You can undo them from Explore's **More** menu.

## 5. Meal trains and dinner parties

A meal train (people taking turns to bring meals) or a dinner party ("A meal with friends") reaches the people you send it to, and anyone they pass it on to. We never see any of it.

- **Live sharing (needs iCloud).** If you're signed in to iCloud and **Sync with iCloud** is on, the app puts every meal train you organise into your private iCloud database and creates a sharing link for it: when you first save the meal train, and when you open a meal train you made earlier. With the switch off it does this only for a meal train you ask to share with **Invite with a link** (section 2). Nobody can open the meal train until the link leaves your device. It leaves in three ways: **Share > Send the link**; **Share > Invite people > Invite with a link** (called **Add someone to it** once the meal train is live); and any invitation message you send or copy while the meal train is live (next point). **Anyone who has the link can open the meal train and sign up**, so send it only to people you mean to invite, and remember it can be forwarded. Everyone on the meal train can see what you entered: who it's for, the dates, how many each meal feeds, what the household can't eat, and your notes and requests. They can also see every sign-up: each person's name, dish and note, and the dish's ingredient names, which are used to check it against what the household can't eat. **Share > Manage who's on it** opens Apple's sharing screen, where the organiser can see and remove the people who joined, and stop sharing. It may show participants' Apple Account names. Once you stop sharing, the app doesn't share that meal train again unless you invite people to it again.
- **An invitation message** (Share > Invite people, then **Send the invitation** or **Copy the message**) says who the meals are for, the open days or courses, how many each meal feeds, what the household can't eat, anything else you've asked people to bring, and your note. Unless the meal train has finished, it ends with a link:
  - **If the meal train is live, the link is its sharing link.** Anyone who gets the message, or has it forwarded to them, can open the whole meal train, including everyone's names and sign-ups, and can sign up themselves.
  - Otherwise, it's a Recimama link that carries the meal train's title, note, dates, servings, dietary needs, courses and requests, but no sign-ups.
- **A meal train file** (Share > Send the meal train) contains the whole meal train, including everyone's sign-ups.
- **Copy as a message** is plain text you paste wherever you like. It gives the meal train's name, what the household can eat, how many it feeds and your note, then each day with the name and dish of whoever signed up for it.

These go wherever you send them (Messages, Mail, AirDrop and so on), and the recipients' apps handle them under their own terms.

## 6. Recipe cards and backups you send

**Share recipe**, **Make a card** and **Settings > Backup > Export a backup** create files: a recipe file, an image or PDF, or a backup of your whole library. They go only where you send or save them, and we never receive them.

## 8. What happens on your device

- **Dictation** is converted to text by Apple's speech recogniser, which the app forces to run on your device. If your device can't transcribe on-device, the app tells you rather than sending your voice to a server.
- **Photos you take or choose** are read on your device. Apple's Vision framework reads the text in them and recognises food. Where Apple Intelligence is available, Apple's on-device model also sorts the lines Vision recognised into the recipe's parts, and on iOS 27 it also looks at the photo itself: for anything the reading missed, or for what dish or food the photo shows. It only suggests. The recipe is written from the photo's own words, and you check what it found, and confirm it, before anything is saved. Nothing leaves your device for any of this. When you choose a photo for a recipe, the app also reads, on your device, whether the file carries a camera's details and whether it is exactly the size of a screen. It keeps only a one-word note of the answer with the recipe (section 1), not the details themselves, and uses it to ask whether you took the photo before it can be published (section 3). Photos and recordings never leave your device, except a card photo you choose to publish (section 3) or send (section 6).
- **PDFs you share to Recimama** are read on your device: their text by Apple's PDFKit, and scanned pages by the same Vision reading as a photo. A PDF never leaves your device. (A link to a PDF is fetched like any other link, section 7.)
- **A video you read a recipe from** is read entirely on your device. You can supply the file yourself, from Photos or Files: **Add a recipe > From a video**, a video you share to Recimama, or "Read it from the video" on an Instagram or TikTok post whose caption has no recipe. **With Recimama Pro, an Instagram post's own video (a reel, or a video in a carousel) can be read too.** On the review of such a post whose caption has the ingredients but not the steps, tap **Read the steps from the video**, and the app fetches the post's public video from Instagram (section 7): only when you tap, never on its own. The video goes into a private temporary file on your device. It is never saved to Photos, shown, shared or uploaded, and it is deleted as soon as the read ends, whether the read finished, failed or you canceled it (if the app is closed during a read, the file is deleted the next time it opens). A private or removed post isn't read, and nor is a post whose video neither of Instagram's pages offers; for those, the review offers Instagram's own Share > Download, where the creator allows it, as before. The app never downloads a video from TikTok, YouTube, Facebook or any other site. Two things are read from a video. Its **sound** is written down by Apple's speech recognition on your device (the first time, your device may download the language's speech model from Apple, as translating a recipe does). The **text it shows on screen** is read by Apple's Vision framework from frames the app samples about once a second. If a caption came with the video, that is read too. Then, where Apple Intelligence is available, Apple's on-device model writes the recipe from those words, and otherwise the app's own rules do; an amount or a time has to appear in the video or its caption, or it is left blank. The video, its sound, the frames and the words written down are never sent anywhere, never uploaded or backed up, and are not kept: only the recipe you save is. A read can be canceled at any point, and videos are read up to 15 minutes long.
- **Watching a post's video for its steps** (Recimama Pro, on iOS 27 with Apple Intelligence). Some videos only show their steps being done: nobody says them and none are written on screen. When reading an Instagram post's video finds no steps, the review offers **Watch the video for the steps**. It fetches the video once more (section 7), picks up to 12 frames from it, each made small (at most 512 pixels on its longest side), and shows them to Apple's on-device model with the caption and the words the read found, asking which cooking steps it can see being done. The frames are made and looked at on your device, in its memory only, and are never saved, sent or kept; the video is deleted when the watch ends, whether it finished, failed or you canceled it. The app then takes out of each step any amount, time or temperature that the caption and the video's words don't give, and marks every step "Seen in the video — check them". Only the steps you save are kept.
- **Apple Intelligence**, where available, runs Apple's on-device model to read recipes, to write the "Invent something" ones and, on iOS 27, to watch a post's video for its steps (above).
- **Translating** a recipe uses Apple's Translation framework on your device. The first time you use a language, your device may download it from Apple.
- **Explore's checks** (the word filter, the copying checks, the photo question and the block-list check, section 3) all run on your device.
