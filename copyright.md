# Copyright on Recimama

*Last updated: 29 September 2026*

Recipes and food photos are somebody's work. Explore, where people share recipes with everyone who uses Recimama, accepts only recipes people wrote themselves, in their own words, with photos they took. Naming the source doesn't make copying allowed ([community guidelines](guidelines.html)). If something on Explore copies your work, tell us and we'll take it down.

## What a notice must contain

Explore can be seen only inside the Recimama app. To help us find what you're complaining about, tell us the recipe's title, the cook's name as Explore shows it, and roughly when you saw it. Include a screenshot if you have one.

Under 17 U.S.C. §512(c)(3), a notice must be in writing and include:

1. **Your signature**, physical or electronic, as the copyright owner or as someone authorised to act for them. In an email, typing your full name is an electronic signature.
2. **The work you say has been copied.** For example, your recipe or your photo, and where you published it. For several works, a representative list is enough.
3. **What on Explore copies it**, with enough detail for us to find it (above).
4. **How to contact you:** your name, postal address, phone number and email address.
5. **A statement that you believe, in good faith,** that the use isn't authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
6. **A statement that the information in your notice is accurate** and, under penalty of perjury, that you're the owner or are authorised to act for the owner.

For 5 and 6 you can copy this: *"I have a good faith belief that use of the material in the manner complained of is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law. The information in this notice is accurate, and under penalty of perjury, I am the owner, or authorized to act on behalf of the owner, of an exclusive right that is allegedly infringed."*

**Before you send one.** Under §512(f), anyone who knowingly and materially misrepresents that something infringes can be liable for the damages that causes. Think about whether what's on Explore really copies you. In the US, a list of ingredients on its own isn't protected by copyright. The written method, the introduction and the photo usually are.

## What happens next

1. **We confirm we've got it**, by reply. We aim to do that within 24 hours.
2. **If your notice is complete, we take the recipe down from Explore promptly.** If something is missing, we tell you what. Copies that people saved to their own libraries before it came down stay with them. We can't reach those.
3. **We record the notice against the cook**, for the repeat-infringer rule below.
4. **We can't tell the cook ourselves.** Recimama has no accounts, so we have no way to reach them. If the cook asks why their recipe was taken down, we may give them a copy of your notice, including your name but not your address, phone number or email address, so that they can reply to it through us.

## If your recipe was taken down by mistake: a counter-notice

If you shared the recipe and believe it was taken down by mistake, or because it was mistaken for something else, you can send a counter-notice. Send it to the agent above, or to [rodrigobayapps@gmail.com](mailto:rodrigobayapps@gmail.com) with "Counter-notice" in the subject. Under 17 U.S.C. §512(g)(3), it must include:

1. **Your signature**, physical or electronic. In an email, typing your full name is an electronic signature.
2. **What was taken down**, and where it appeared: the recipe's title, the name you shared it under, and roughly when you shared it.
3. **A statement, under penalty of perjury,** that you believe in good faith the recipe was taken down as a result of a mistake or of misidentification.
4. **Your name, postal address and phone number**, and a statement that:
   - you consent to the jurisdiction of the US federal district court for the district where your address is, or, if your address is outside the US, of any judicial district in which we may be found
   - you'll accept service of process from the person who sent the notice, or from their agent

**What we do with it.** We send a copy of your counter-notice, **including your name and address**, to the person who sent the notice. We tell them the recipe can go back up in 10 business days. The recipe can then go back up between 10 and 14 business days after we received your counter-notice. That happens unless, before then, they tell us they've filed a court action to stop you. If they don't, we lift any block the notice caused and tell you. The notice then no longer counts against you.

A taken-down recipe is deleted from Explore, and you still have it in your own library. So "going back up" means you share it again, and we'll tell you when you can.

## Repeat infringers

**A cook named in two valid copyright notices is blocked from Explore, and everything they've shared is removed.** The app then won't let them share again. We count notices, not recipes: one notice naming several of a cook's recipes is one notice. These don't count:

- a notice that is withdrawn
- a notice answered by a valid counter-notice that wasn't followed by a court action

We handle intellectual-property claims that Apple forwards to us in the same way.
