## 4. How we moderate Explore

This section is how we keep Explore within its rules. It is what the EU Digital Services Act (Article 14) and the UK Online Safety Act ask terms like these to explain.

### Checks the app makes, on your device

The app runs these checks automatically. None of them sends anything to us.

- **The word filter.** A short list of words is built into the app: slurs, explicit sexual terms, and terms that promote eating disorders. It matches whole words (and, for a few entries, the start of a word), never the middle of one.
  - When you share, it checks the title, description, ingredients, steps, tags, your name and your note. It refuses a recipe that contains one of the words, and tells you which part to change.
  - When Explore is shown, every copy of the app checks each recipe the same way, and doesn't show one that fails.
  - Because it is a list, it sometimes refuses something innocent. Change the word, or email us.
- **The copying checks.** The app refuses a recipe whose history shows it came from somewhere else: imported from a website, read from a photographed page, from Explore or another person, or following a recipe published online. It also refuses pasted text with signs of copying in it: a web address, ©, "All rights reserved", "Jump to recipe", "Print recipe", "Nutrition Facts" or "mealime".
- **The photo question.** If a photo, when you chose it, carried no camera details or was exactly the size of a screen, the app asks whether you took it before it can be shared.
- **The block list.** The app checks that you aren't blocked before you share. It also hides blocked cooks' recipes.

**What these checks can and can't do.** The app's automatic checks (the word filter, the copying checks, the photo question and the block-list check) can only refuse a recipe before it's shared, or keep a listing from being shown. None of them takes a published recipe down or blocks a cook. A person does that (below).

**The technology the UK Online Safety Act asks us to describe.** The Act calls technology that looks through content for harmful material "proactive technology". Recimama uses two kinds, and this section says what each is, when it runs and how it works. The first is the word filter above. The second is photo fingerprints:

- When you share a recipe with a photo, your copy of the app works out a fingerprint of the photo, a short code from which the photo can't be recreated, and saves it with the recipe on Explore.
- When a person reports an intimate image of themselves, we use that fingerprint to look for other recipes on Explore with exactly the same photo. Before treating one as a copy, the moderator's copy of the app works out its fingerprint again from the photo itself.
- It finds only identical copies, not a photo that was cropped, edited or saved again. A person sees the copies it finds and decides what to take down.

The copying checks and the photo question are about copyright, and the block-list check is about who is sharing. They don't look for harmful content.

## 8. Cooking safety

**Times, nutrition and dietary flags are estimates.** Please read this.

- **Times.** Recimama's timelines, step times, alerts and finishing times are estimates, worked out from the recipe's words and from how you've cooked before. **Recimama can't see your pan.** Check that meat, poultry, eggs and rice are cooked through, by their look and temperature, not by the timer. For example, cook poultry to 165°F (74°C).
- **Alerts** can be late or not arrive, for example if notifications are off, the device is silenced or the battery runs out. Don't leave cooking unattended because an alert is set.
- **Nutrition** is an estimate from average ingredient values. It is not dietary or medical advice.
- **Dietary flags**, such as a recipe containing nuts or a dish suiting a guest's needs, come from matching words in the recipe. They can miss things: an ingredient hidden in a product, cross-contamination, or a word the app doesn't know. Never rely on them for an allergy or a medical condition. Check every ingredient and label yourself.
- **Recipes from Explore** were written by other people, and we don't test them. Somebody else wrote them: check times and temperatures.
- **Recipes invented with Apple Intelligence** were written by a computer that can't taste or see anything, and it can be wrong. Recimama wrote this: check the food, not just the timer.
- **Preparing the night before:** cool cooked food and refrigerate it within 2 hours, keep raw meat covered in the fridge, and reheat to 165°F (74°C).

You decide how you cook and what you serve.
